# itch.io Devlog draft — VOLT NOMAD Release Candidate

## Title

VOLT NOMAD has reached its release candidate

## Body

VOLT NOMAD started as three very different prototypes built around the AI Browser Game Jam 4 theme, **CHARGE**. The version on itch.io now is the result of repeatedly throwing away what was merely functional and keeping what survived real playtests.

The final direction is a compact incremental boss rush. Every manual command attacks and generates CHARGE; that same resource grows five interconnected gear trees until your automatic arsenal takes over. Three of six machine beasts lead to a short normal ending, while the complete route continues through every remaining core, two abyssal bosses, ARCH SINGULARITY, and an optional three-form final confrontation.

The current release candidate includes:

- six order-independent machine-beast hunts and five simultaneous gear trees;
- 86 upgrade nodes and 317 standard ranks;
- 33 bilingual story events and a permanent conversation archive;
- individual combat music for every enemy and every final form;
- persistent saves, achievements, credits, Infinite Mode, and a 14-piece artwork archive;
- mouse, keyboard, touch, and gamepad support;
- English and Japanese text;
- a true-ending cinematic and dedicated ending music.

This was an AI-heavy but human-directed production. Codex supported implementation, balancing, writing, tests, and production; PixelLab produced reviewed pixel-art candidates; OpenAI image generation produced cinematic illustrations; and Suno produced music candidates. Takahiro Saeki directed the game, played every major iteration, rejected and selected assets, and made the final design decisions.

Before this release candidate, the automated suite completed all **1,440 campaign permutations**, validated 33 Japanese/English story records, exercised save recovery, and checked the packaged music and defeat effects. The remaining work is a fresh human playthrough of the actual itch.io upload.

If you play it, the most useful feedback is simple: when did the build first feel powerful, when did the next action become unclear, and where did the game turn into repetition?
